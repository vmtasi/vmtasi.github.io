Copperbelt Mineral Prospectivity

Bayesian spatial modelling of Cu–Co mineralization using public regional geological and geophysical data.

Question

What geological variables provide spatially meaningful controls on Cu–Co mineralization in the Central African Copperbelt, and how much of that signal can be recovered from public regional data?

Approach

The project combines GIS-based spatial feature engineering with Bayesian logistic modelling. The legacy modelling grid contains 1,899 cells; 139 cells carry a positive label. That grid is a historical modelling frame, not the adopted study area. Predictors include distance to lithological contacts, distance to faults, regional Bouguer gravity, lithological classes, and tectonic-domain information derived from the Katangan Basin interpretation of Daly et al.

Model evaluation uses spatial block cross-validation rather than random train/test splitting, because nearby observations are not independent and random splitting can produce overly optimistic estimates of generalization.

What the project taught me

A particularly important result was methodological rather than numerical. Aeromagnetic coverage contained large spatial gaps that coincided with areas carrying positive training labels. This created a pathway for a model to learn the geometry of survey coverage instead of a geological magnetic signal.

Evidence status

The current phase is a source-level evidence harvest, not a model update. The acquired corpus contains 118 documents; 1,872 geochemical measurement rows have been extracted from six of them, together with 24 radiometric summaries, 19 drill holes and a 118-row document register. The RDC.ROCKS occurrence set remains 147 observations, 113 provisional candidate entities and 0 confirmed cross-source identities. No RDC.ROCKS observation is a model positive.

The study area is not yet decided: the Daly union, the 1,899-cell V14 grid and the five permissive tracts are historical frames, not an adopted boundary.

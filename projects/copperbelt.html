Copperbelt Mineral Prospectivity

Bayesian spatial modelling of Cu–Co mineralization using public regional geological and geophysical data.

Question

What geological variables provide spatially meaningful controls on Cu–Co mineralization in the Central African Copperbelt, and how much of that signal can be recovered from public regional data?

Approach

The project combines GIS-based spatial feature engineering with Bayesian logistic modelling. The study grid contains 1,899 cells and 139 known deposits. Predictors include distance to lithological contacts, distance to faults, regional Bouguer gravity, lithological classes, and tectonic-domain information derived from the Katangan Basin interpretation of Daly et al.

Model evaluation uses spatial block cross-validation rather than random train/test splitting, because nearby observations are not independent and random splitting can produce overly optimistic estimates of generalization.

What the project taught me

A particularly important result was methodological rather than numerical. Aeromagnetic coverage contained large spatial gaps that coincided with areas containing known deposits. This created a pathway for a model to learn the geometry of survey coverage instead of a geological magnetic signal.

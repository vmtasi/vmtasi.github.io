OBS-01 · Status: open

Aeromagnetic coverage is an observation-process boundary

Evidence. 819 of 1,899 cells fall in the EMAG2v3 nodata footprint. 91 of the 139 label-positive cells fall in that gap.

Implication. A model can learn the survey geometry and report it as geology. The gap belongs to the observation process, not to the geological signal.

OBS-02 · Status: open

Predicted probability is calibrated in the large, not locally

Evidence. The V9 surface has a maximum of 0.25 and a mean of 0.068, against a prevalence of 0.073. The mean matches the base rate.

Implication. Matching the base rate is calibration in the large. It is not evidence that local probabilities are reliable. The ranking is the weak part.

OBS-03 · Status: open

AUC and average precision answer different questions

Evidence. The aggregate spatial AUC is 0.693. Average precision is low in absolute terms; the no-skill value is 0.073. At a list of 50 cells, chance captures 3.7 of the 139 label-positive cells.

Implication. AUC weights every positive-negative pair and is dominated by easy negatives. Average precision concentrates on the top of the list, which is the part a drilling decision uses. Report capture at the decision list length, not AUC alone.

OBS-04 · Status: open

The study area is not yet decided

Evidence. The Daly union was an investigated candidate framework, not an adopted boundary. The 1,899-cell V14 grid is a historical modelling frame. The five permissive tracts are historical. The acquired evidence clusters in the Kolwezi–Tenke–Likasi–Lubumbashi corridor, and most analytical samples have no per-sample coordinates.

Implication. None of these extents should be presented as the study-area boundary. The boundary decision remains open and must be justified from the geological and metallogenic evidence.

OBS-05 · Status: open

Spatial validation tests transfer, not interpolation

Evidence. The V9 aggregate AUC is 0.693; per-block values differ. Random cross-validation asks whether the model interpolates. Spatial block cross-validation asks whether it transfers.

Implication. Report per-block performance. A model that scores 0.75 in four blocks and 0.55 in one has a transfer problem; a model that scores 0.69 everywhere has a ceiling problem. Block size should follow deposit spacing and the range of spatial dependence, not the bounding box.

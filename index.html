Questions

  • What does it mean for a model to understand something?
  • Can prediction exist without explanation?
  • How much of a dataset describes the world, and how much describes the way we observed it?
  • Can machines discover structures that humans systematically overlook?
  • Does more computation produce more knowledge, or merely a larger space to search?
  • What should humans do when machines become better at inference than at deciding what is worth knowing?

Principles

  • A model is not an explanation.
  • Prediction without validation is speculation.
  • Missing data is information about the observation process.
  • Every feature should have a reason to exist.
  • If a model performs surprisingly well, investigate why before celebrating.
  • Computation is a tool for thinking, not a substitute for thinking.

About

I am a data scientist interested in applying statistical and computational methods to physical and spatial systems. My current work is moving toward scientific machine learning, Bayesian inference, geospatial modelling, and mineral exploration.

I like working from first principles, taking abstractions apart when necessary, and treating models as hypotheses rather than authorities.

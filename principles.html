Principles

Principles, loosely defined and incomplete.

Data & Modeling

  • Quantify the uncertainty. An estimate without a confidence boundary is just a guess.
  • Spatial data is never independent. Always use spatial block cross-validation.
  • Define the constraints before you try to maximize the objective.
  • Plot the raw data. Look at the noise before you try to fit a curve to it.
  • If the math doesn’t survive physical logistics and hard economics, the math is wrong.
  • Extract the signal, but never pretend the noise isn't there.

Code & Execution

  • Seed every random state. Reproducibility is the baseline, not a feature.
  • Typography is not an afterthought. Never settle for default LaTeX typography; take control of your presentation (and always use Consolas for code).
  • Clear the cell outputs before committing the Jupyter notebook.
  • Use physics-informed models when the physical laws are known. Don't make a neural network relearn gravity or electromagnetism from scratch.

Philosophy & Life

  • Respect what has endured. Classic texts and old structures survived the test of time for a reason.
  • Deal with the physical reality. Pure theory means nothing if it can't be deployed.
  • Read Chateaubriand.
  • Build on foundations instead of tearing them down.
